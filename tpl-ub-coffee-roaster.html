{{t55|Thursday is}}{{t13|Roast day}}{{t56|Four steps, one ticket per batch}}

{{t57|Every batch gets a paper ticket when the green coffee is weighed, and a stamp at each step until it is bagged. The ticket stays pinned above the drum until the last bag is sold.}}

  1. {{t58|Tuesday}}

    {{t59|Weighed out}}

    {{t60|Green coffee comes off the pallet in twelve-kilo lots. We pick out broken and pale beans by hand on the back table.}}

  2. {{t61|Thursday, 7 am}}

    {{t62|Into the drum}}

    {{t63|The drum is charged hot and each batch takes eleven to thirteen minutes. We listen for first crack and drop every coffee by its own clock.}}

  3. {{t64|Four minutes later}}

    {{t65|Stirred cool}}

    {{t66|The tray's arms turn the beans until they are cool to the hand. Fast cooling keeps the sweetness the drum built.}}

  4. {{t67|Friday}}

    {{t68|Bagged and dated}}

    {{t69|Each bag gets the batch number and the roast date. The beans rest a day on the shelf, then they are yours.}}

{{t152|Asked at}}{{t9|The counter}}{{t153|Five things people ask}}

{{t154|Can you grind for my brewer?}}

{{t155|Yes — tell us what you brew with, from espresso to cafetière, and we grind on the spot. Whole beans keep longer, so we will always offer that first.}}

{{t156|How long do the beans keep?}}

{{t157|They are at their best from a few days to five weeks after the roast date on the bag. Keep them sealed, cool and out of the fridge.}}

{{t158|Do you post beans?}}

{{t159|Orders placed by Wednesday night go out on Friday with the new roast. Nigeria postage is free over two bags.}}

{{t160|Can I have a regular delivery?}}

{{t161|Every two or four weeks, whichever coffee you like or the roaster's pick. Pause or stop it from the email we send before each roast.}}

{{t162|Do you supply cafés and offices?}}

{{t163|We roast for a few cafés and offices in Ibadan, delivered by bike in returnable tins. Ask at the counter or send us an email.}}
