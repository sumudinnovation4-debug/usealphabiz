{{t27|A customs house of 1908}}

{{t28|For sixty years the Dogana weighed salt, wine and almonds at the end of the mole. We kept its vaults, its ledger shelves and the iron rings in the wall, and opened eleven rooms above them.}}

{{t32|The walls are ninety centimetres thick, which is why the rooms stay cool in August without machines. The floors are the terracotta tiles of the original roof, turned over and laid again.}}

{{t33|The ledger shelves on the ground floor now hold the books guests leave behind, and the old weighing tower is the one suite. Everything else is linen, lime and the light off the water.}}

  1. {{t59|07:00}}

    {{t60|The shutters open}}

    {{t61|The first ferry sounds once. Coffee, ricotta and bread from the oven on Via del Forno wait under the vaults until noon; nobody asks when you came down.}}

  2. {{t62|09:30}}

    {{t63|The boats come in}}

    {{t64|The Maria Grazia ties up under the sea rooms. Rosa walks the mole with a crate and decides what lunch will be.}}

  3. {{t65|12:00}}

    {{t66|Down the ladder}}

    {{t67|An iron ladder goes down the harbour wall into eight metres of clear water. Towels wait on the landing; the sea is warmest at noon.}}

  4. {{t68|15:00}}

    {{t69|The long hour}}

    {{t70|Awnings come down, the town closes and so does the house. A hammock on the roof, a book from the ledger shelves, the key to the cold cellar.}}

  5. {{t71|18:30}}

    {{t72|The wind turns}}

    {{t73|The ostro drops at dusk and the swallows come out. Cold white wine on the roof, olives from the hill behind the town.}}

  6. {{t74|21:00}}

    {{t75|Lamps on the mole}}

    {{t76|Eleven tables along the harbour wall, one lamp each. The kitchen closes when the last table is ready to go, not before.}}

{{t121|Before you come}}

{{t122|Anything not answered here, ask Davide at the desk or by telephone on}} {{t15|+39 ⟦phone⟧}}{{t123|, any day from eight to eight.}}

{{t124|When are check-in and check-out?}}

{{t125|Rooms are ready from two in the afternoon. Leave when the ferry or the train suits you; bags wait on the ground floor as long as you need.}}

{{t126|Where do we leave the car?}}

{{t127|The old town is closed to cars. Leave yours at the station car park for €12 a day; we collect you and your bags with the hand cart, which is quicker than it sounds.}}

{{t128|Can we bring children or a dog?}}

{{t129|Children are welcome in the harbour rooms and the tower. Well-mannered dogs stay in the harbour rooms for €20 a night.}}

{{t130|Is the house step-free?}}

{{t131|One harbour room on the first floor is reached by lift and has a step-free shower. The roof terrace and the ladder are reached by stairs only.}}

{{t132|When is the house open?}}

{{t133|From the middle of March to the start of November. In winter the kitchen closes and the masons come back.}}

{{t134|Can we come for dinner without staying?}}

{{t135|Guests of the house have a table every night. Others can write to us after two in the afternoon for the tables left on the mole.}}

{{t136|What if our plans change?}}

{{t137|Cancel free of charge until fourteen days before you arrive; after that we keep the first night.}}
