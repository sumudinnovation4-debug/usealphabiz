({{t93|Since 1936}})

{{t94|Built by the town, saved by its swimmers.}}

{{t95|Hartfield Lido opened in the summer of 1936 with a brass band and a swimming gala. It closed in 1989 when the boiler gave up, sat empty for fifteen summers, and reopened in 2004 after the Friends of Hartfield Lido raised the money to mend it, a good part of it in fivers at bake sales.}}

  1. {{t96|1936}}{{t97|Opened by the borough, free on Sunday mornings.}}
  2. {{t98|1989}}{{t99|Closed when the boiler broke and the council could not replace it.}}
  3. {{t100|2004}}{{t101|Reopened by the Friends, a charity run by its swimmers.}}
  4. {{t102|2019}}{{t103|Solar panels on the roof, and swimming all year round.}}
{{/sec_story}} {{#sec_nights}}

{{t156|Good to know}}

{{t157|Anything else? Call the front desk on}} {{t153|⟦phone⟧}} {{t158|or ask at the kiosk hatch.}}

{{t159|Do I need to book?}}

{{t160|Booking guarantees your place, but a third of every session is kept for walk-ups. On grey days there is nearly always room.}}

{{t161|How warm is the water?}}

{{t162|From May to September the solar roof keeps it at about 24 degrees. In winter it follows the weather, usually between 4 and 12 degrees.}}

{{t163|Can children swim on their own?}}

{{t164|From age eight, if they can swim 25 metres. Younger children swim with an adult in the water, one adult for two children.}}

{{t165|Are there lockers and showers?}}

{{t166|Yes: lockers that take a pound coin and return it, warm showers, and a changing village with family cubicles.}}

{{t167|Is the lido accessible?}}

{{t168|There is step-free access from the gate to the deck, a pool hoist at the shallow end and an accessible changing room. Carers swim free.}}

{{t169|Can I bring my own food?}}

{{t170|Of course. Picnics are welcome on the lawn; we only ask that glass stays at home.}}
