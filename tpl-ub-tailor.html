{{t36|Three fittings, twelve weeks}}

{{t37|No coat leaves the workroom on a first try. Each fitting moves it closer to the way you stand, sit and reach, and each is booked around your diary.}}

  1. {{t38|Measure}}

    {{t39|Step 1}}{{t40|Twenty-six measures and notes on how you stand; your pattern is drafted by hand that week.}}

  2. {{t41|Baste}}

    {{t42|Step 2}}{{t43|A coat held together with loose white stitches, opened and chalked again on you.}}

  3. {{t44|Forward}}

    {{t45|Step 3}}{{t46|Pockets, lapels and sleeves in place; the balance and the collar are set.}}

  4. {{t47|Finish}}

    {{t48|Step 4}}{{t49|Hand-sewn buttonholes, a last press, and the suit goes home with you.}}

{{t110|How long does a suit take?}}

{{t111|About twelve weeks from the first measure, with three fittings three to four weeks apart. A second suit from the same pattern takes around eight.}}

{{t112|Do I need to come to Enugu?}}

{{t113|No. Our trunk shows bring the cloth and the fittings to Lagos, Lagos, Copenhagen, New York and Kaduna twice a year.}}

{{t114|What makes it bespoke rather than made to measure?}}

{{t115|Your pattern is drawn from nothing for you, not adjusted from a standard block, and the coat is basted and refitted on you before it is finished.}}

{{t116|Can you copy a suit I already own?}}

{{t117|We can take its details as a starting point, but we always cut a new pattern for you rather than tracing the old one.}}

{{t118|Do you make for women?}}

{{t119|Yes. About a third of the cutting book is suits, coats and trousers for women, cut from the same house style.}}
