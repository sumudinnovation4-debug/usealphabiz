{{t16|Soil first. Everything else follows.}}

{{t17|Four rules every ⟦name⟧ garden is built on, whether it is a courtyard behind a terrace or three acres on the edge of the Downs.}}

  1. {{t18|Feed the soil, not the plant.}}

    {{t19|We mulch with compost made from our own green waste, never peat, and leave out chemical feeds. Living soil holds water, stores carbon and does the feeding for you.}}

    {{t20|Compost}}

  2. {{t21|Keep every drop that falls.}}

    {{t22|Rain gardens, gravel instead of tarmac and a water butt on every downpipe. A ⟦name⟧ garden sends almost nothing into the drain and needs little watering by its second summer.}}

    {{t23|Rainwater}}

  3. {{t24|Plant for the whole food web.}}

    {{t25|Native hedges, borders that flower from March to November and seedheads left standing through winter: food and shelter in every month of the year.}}

    {{t26|Habitat}}

  4. {{t27|Build from close by.}}

    {{t28|Sussex flint, local oak and reclaimed brick from within thirty miles. No plastic edging, no imported stone, nothing that will not age well.}}

    {{t29|Local}}

{{t90|Aftercare, all year round}}

{{t91|Most new gardens fail in their third year, when nobody is looking. Ours are visited every season by the people who planted them.}}

{{t92|Spring}}

{{t93|March to May · three visits}}

  • {{t94|Cut back last year's stems once the insects have left them}}
  • {{t95|Mulch every bed with compost from our yard}}
  • {{t96|Lift and divide crowded perennials}}

{{t97|Summer}}

{{t98|June to August · two visits}}

  • {{t99|Cut meadow paths and the first hay in late July}}
  • {{t100|Deadhead for a second flush, leave the rest to seed}}
  • {{t101|Check ponds, butts and new trees through dry spells}}

{{t102|Autumn}}

{{t103|September to November · two visits}}

  • {{t104|Plant bulbs, bare-root hedging and new trees}}
  • {{t105|Rake leaves onto the beds instead of the bonfire}}
  • {{t106|Sow yellow rattle into lawns turning to meadow}}

{{t107|Winter}}

{{t108|December to February · one visit}}

  • {{t109|Prune fruit trees and lay the hedges}}
  • {{t110|Leave seedheads and hollow stems standing}}
  • {{t111|Walk the garden with you and plan the year}}

{{t123|Questions we are asked}}

{{t124|Anything else, ask us on the walk round. The first visit is free within twenty miles of Lewes.}}

{{t125|How much does a new garden cost?}}

{{t126|Most of our builds fall between ₦9,000,000 and ₦45,000,000. Design on its own starts at ₦700,000 for a small garden, and the fee comes off the build if we make it.}}

{{t127|Where do you work?}}

{{t128|Within about forty minutes of our yard in Lewes: Ikoyi and Hove, Seaford, Uckfield and the edge of the Weald. Aftercare keeps us close, so we do not travel further.}}

{{t129|How long until it looks established?}}

{{t130|Borders fill out in their second summer; hedges and trees take three to five years. We plan the first year so the garden never looks bare.}}

{{t131|Can you take over a garden someone else made?}}

{{t132|Yes. We look after it as it is for a full season first, then suggest changes once we know what thrives there.}}

{{t133|Will you reuse what is already there?}}

{{t134|Always, if it is healthy. Soil, stone, paving and plants on site are used before anything new is bought.}}
